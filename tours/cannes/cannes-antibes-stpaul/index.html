The Tour

We pick you up at your hotel and start with Cannes itself — which most visitors never properly see because they never leave the Croisette. Le Suquet, the old town on the hill above the port, is a five-minute drive from the Carlton and a different century. The Villa Rothschild, the marché Forville, the Allée des Étoiles outside the Palais. Then east along the coast through Juan-les-Pins to Cap d'Antibes — one of the most expensive peninsulas in Europe and one of the least photographed. Antibes old town: Provençal baroque, the ramparts Picasso painted, the Château Grimaldi where he worked in 1946. From Antibes we climb inland to Saint-Paul-de-Vence — a medieval village on a ridge at 180 metres with the Fondation Maeght ten minutes outside the walls. Three stops, one day, and a Riviera that begins five hundred metres from your hotel.

The route.
Three stops, one day.

01
Cannes
From your hotel · allow 60 min

We start at the Villa Rothschild, built in 1881 by Élisabeth de Rothschild. The park is formal and contained — terraces, Mediterranean plantings, the Belle Époque villa above it — and tucked behind the port hill, away from the bay. Then Le Suquet, the old town on the hill above the port. From up here you see Cannes as it actually is: the Croisette curved below like a parenthesis, the Îles de Lérins offshore. At the foot of the hill, the marché Forville — a covered market, open every morning, the kind of place where the restaurants still shop. We walk down through it. The Palais des Festivals next — not a beautiful building, which is part of its honesty. What you come for is the Allée des Étoiles: the handprints and signatures of the film world set in the pavement outside, and the specific quality of red carpet underfoot. Then the Croisette — but from the other direction, on foot, the way it was meant to be seen. Five palace hotels in succession, each from a different decade, each a different idea of what luxury looked like at the time.

Most guests skip this: Almost everyone on La Croisette walks between the Carlton and the Palais and calls it Cannes. Le Suquet, the market, the Villa Rothschild — these are five minutes away and a different city. We start here because it is the honest version of where you are staying.
02
Antibes
~20 min from Cannes · allow 90–120 min

From Cannes we drive east along the coast, passing through Juan-les-Pins — the jazz festival town, flat and relaxed where Cannes is vertical and performative. Then the Cap d'Antibes. The road circles the peninsula under umbrella pines and past the gates of estates that are never photographed: this is where the serious money has always been private. The south side of the cap brings you to Port de l'Olivette — a small bay, fishing boats in faded primary colours pulled up against the stone wall. No beach bar, no organised views, nobody selling anything. The kind of place that painters found before anyone else did, and that still looks exactly as it did then. After the Cap, we park at Port Vauban — the largest yacht port on the Mediterranean — and you walk from there. The old town is compact: Provençal baroque, the covered market open every morning, the ramparts above the sea. The Château Grimaldi above the ramparts now houses the Musée Picasso (€8) — the rooms where he worked in 1946, still hung with the work he made here.

Musée Picasso or the market? If the market is open (every morning except Monday), start there. Then the old town, the ramparts, and the museum. If you are choosing between the two, the museum is better on a second visit to Antibes; the old town and the Cap are the first visit. Tell us your preference and we adjust the timing.
03
Saint-Paul-de-Vence
~25 min from Antibes · allow 60–90 min

From Antibes we leave the coast and climb into the hills of the arrière-pays. Saint-Paul-de-Vence is a medieval village on a ridge at 180 metres, surrounded by ramparts that have changed very little since the sixteenth century. We leave you at the main gate; the car waits below. Inside: a single main street, stone fountains, a twelfth-century church, and the cemetery where Chagall is buried. The village is pedestrian and small enough to walk end-to-end in twenty minutes, which means the hour you have here is for looking rather than moving. The Fondation Maeght is ten minutes’ walk outside the walls — one of the best modern art collections in France, housed in a building by Josep Lluís Sert (€22, allow an hour). If you go there, do it first. The return to Cannes takes approximately thirty minutes via the autoroute, or forty-five through the hills if you prefer the scenic route.

Fondation Maeght or the village? Not easily both in one hour. The Fondation has Miró, Giacometti, Braque, Calder — serious work in a serious building. The village has atmosphere. If you want both, allow ninety minutes for this stop. Tell us your preference before we leave Antibes.

Questions.
Plain answers.

How long is this tour?

From six hours for all three stops. We start with Cannes itself, then Antibes is twenty minutes east; Saint-Paul is another twenty-five minutes inland. You can extend to a full day — add lunch at La Colombe d'Or or more time exploring Cap d'Antibes. We pick you up at your hotel and return you there.

Where do you pick us up?

At your hotel in Cannes, or any address you choose. We also pick up from Mougins, Le Cannet, Mandelieu, Antibes and Juan-les-Pins at no extra charge. If you are staying in Antibes itself, we start with Cap d'Antibes and adjust the route accordingly.

Can we visit the Musée Picasso?

Yes. The Musée Picasso is inside the Château Grimaldi in the old town, directly above the sea. Entry is around €8. Allow forty-five minutes. Mention it when you book and we allow the right amount of time.

Is the Fondation Maeght worth the time?

Yes, if modern art is of any interest. The permanent collection includes major works by Miró, Giacometti, Braque, Léger and Calder, in a building purpose-designed for them. It is not a compromise collection. Budget sixty to seventy-five minutes and go first, before the village.

Is Saint-Paul suitable for limited mobility?

The main street is cobbled but manageable. The upper lanes are steeper. The Fondation Maeght is fully accessible and is the better choice if walking is difficult. In Antibes, the old town is mostly flat; the Cap d'Antibes circuit is entirely by car.

Can we add Grasse or Nice?

Yes. Grasse and Gourdon are thirty minutes from Saint-Paul — we can combine both routes into a full day. Adding Nice is also possible but makes for a long day; we would recommend our Nice · Eze · Monaco tour instead. Same price either way.
