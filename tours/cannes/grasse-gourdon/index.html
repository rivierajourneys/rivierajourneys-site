The Tour

We pick you up at your hotel in Cannes and drive north. Twenty-five kilometres and four hundred metres of altitude later, the sea is behind you and the Provence hinterland begins. Grasse is the perfume capital of the world — not a marketing claim but a geographical fact, because the flowers that fix scent better than anything in the Mediterranean happen to grow on these specific hillsides. Above Grasse, the Gorges du Loup cuts through the limestone massif to Gourdon: an eagle's nest village at 760 metres with a view that takes in the entire coastline. The road back descends through the gorge to Tourrettes-sur-Loup, the violet village, where artisan workshops occupy the medieval lanes. Four stops, one day, a Riviera that most visitors never see because they never leave the coast.

The route.
Four stops, one day.

01
Grasse
~30 min from Cannes · allow 60–75 min

We leave Cannes heading north on the D6085 — the road climbs steadily from the coast, the sea appearing behind you in the rear mirror as the olive groves and limestone outcrops begin. Grasse is twenty-five kilometres and four hundred metres above sea level. The town has been making perfume since the sixteenth century, when Catalan glove-makers discovered that the flowers growing on these hillsides — rose, jasmine, mimosa, violet — fixed scent better than anything known in the Mediterranean. The perfume industry never really left. Fragonard, Molinard, Galimard: all three still operate in the old town and offer free guided tours of their ateliers, usually forty-five minutes, genuinely worth doing. The old town itself is Provençal baroque — steep lanes, ochre facades, the cathedral with three Rubens paintings that almost nobody knows about. The market square in the morning has the best cheese in the region.

Our recommendation: Book the Fragonard or Molinard tour in advance in July and August — they fill up. Tell us when you enquire and we will allow the right amount of time. If perfumeries are not of interest, forty-five minutes in the old town and the market is enough.
02
Gourdon
~15 min from Grasse · allow 40–50 min

From Grasse we take the D3 into the Gorges du Loup — a canyon road that cuts through the limestone massif above the town. Gourdon sits on the absolute edge of a cliff at 760 metres: a medieval village on a spur of rock with a vertical fall of several hundred metres on three sides. The only approach is on foot from the car park, a five-minute walk. Inside the walls: forty or so stone houses, a twelfth-century château, terraced gardens originally designed by Le Nôtre, and a view that takes in the entire coastline from the Estérel to the Italian border on a clear day. The village is small enough to walk in twenty minutes; the view is why you came.

The view: Best in the morning, before haze builds over the sea. On clear days in winter and spring you can see Cannes, Antibes, Nice, Cap Ferrat, and the Alps behind them. The café in the main square serves the view with the coffee.
03
Saut du Loup
~10 min from Gourdon · allow 20–30 min

Descending from Gourdon through the gorge, we stop at the Cascade du Saut du Loup — a series of waterfalls where the Loup river has cut through the limestone over millennia, forming pools and travertine shelves stained ochre and green by the minerals in the water. The falls are accessible by a short path from the road; in spring and after rain they are substantial. The name translates as the Wolf's Leap — the river drops far enough that the legend writes itself. The contrast with the dry limestone of Gourdon above is striking.

04
Tourrettes-sur-Loup
~10 min from Saut du Loup · allow 45–60 min

The last stop is Tourrettes-sur-Loup, a medieval village on a ridge above the Loup valley, known as the city of violets. The village has been cultivating violets commercially since the nineteenth century — for perfume, for crystallised petals, for violet syrup — and in February and March the hillsides below the ramparts are planted with them. The medieval centre is a single looping street lined with artisan workshops: potters, weavers, jewellers, painters. Not a tourist approximation — these are working ateliers, most of them there because the rent is lower than on the coast and the light is better. The return to Cannes takes approximately 35 minutes via the D6085.

What to buy: Violet products from the local producers — the crystallised violets in particular are not found elsewhere. The village cooperative sells direct. The artisan workshops are worth twenty minutes of your time regardless of whether you buy anything.

Questions.
Plain answers.

How long is this tour?

From five hours for all four stops. You can extend to a full day if you want more time in Grasse or add lunch in the hills — there are excellent restaurants in Tourrettes and Gourdon. We pick you up at your hotel in Cannes and return you there.

Where do you pick us up?

At your hotel in Cannes, or any address you choose. We can also arrange pickup from hotels in Mougins, Le Cannet, Mandelieu, Antibes or Juan-les-Pins. If you are staying outside Cannes, mention it when you enquire — there is no additional charge.

Can we visit a perfumery in Grasse?

Yes. Fragonard, Molinard and Galimard all offer free guided tours of their ateliers, usually forty-five minutes. Tell us when you enquire and we allow the right amount of time. Book in advance in July and August — they fill up.

Is Gourdon suitable for people with limited mobility?

The car park is a five-minute walk from the village on a paved path. The village itself is small and mostly flat. Grasse old town has steeper lanes but the perfumeries are fully accessible. We can adjust the itinerary to suit your needs.

Is this tour good for children?

Yes. The Gorges du Loup waterfalls and Gourdon's cliff-edge views are popular with children. The perfumery tours are less interesting for young children but the Grasse market and Tourrettes artisan workshops usually hold their attention. Child seats available on request at no charge.

Can we add Saint-Paul-de-Vence?

Yes. Saint-Paul-de-Vence is twenty minutes from Tourrettes — it adds about ninety minutes to the day. We usually visit it after Tourrettes on the way back to Cannes. If you want both, allow seven hours total. Same price.
