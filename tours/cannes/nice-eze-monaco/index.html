The Tour

It sounds counterintuitive, but this tour is the most efficient thing you will do on the Riviera. If this is your first time on the coast — and particularly if you are based in Cannes, where the pleasures are considerable but geographically limited — one day east is all it takes to see the French Riviera between here and Monaco, and to return with the sort of informed opinion that people expect you to have. Someone will ask about Eze. They always do. Or perhaps you have seen it all yourself, but you are travelling with children, friends, or in-laws who have not — and who will reasonably expect to be shown Monaco.

The alternative is three days. You could drive it yourself, or take the train, or assemble some combination of buses and goodwill — but however you attempt it, it is three days. And Eze by train is essentially a theoretical proposition involving a station at the bottom of a mountain and a walk that Friedrich Nietzsche considered character-forming.

We collect you from your hotel in a Mercedes V-Class — or wherever you happen to be staying — at a civilised hour, drive a route we have refined over several hundred iterations, and proceed at a pace that allows you to actually see things rather than merely photograph them. There will be conversation. There will almost certainly be lunch. There may be wine. By late afternoon you are back, and tomorrow Cannes can have you again, rested and arguably more interesting for having been away.

The route.
Four stops, one day.

01
Nice
~45 min from Cannes · 60 min ashore

We leave Cannes on the A8 and in roughly forty minutes reach Nice — along the Promenade des Anglais, the most pleasant of all possible approaches, into the old town. Cours Saleya. Niçoise baroque — lavishly decorated churches in what was once a poor fishing town. A not-quite-secret but largely unknown proper way to climb from the old town to the hill above it, with a stop for coffee where it matters. The view of the city and the sea from above. And an equally pleasant descent back to the Promenade. One hour and you will have seen more of Nice than the average resident manages in forty years.

02
Moyenne Corniche
2 panoramic stops en route to Eze

From Nice we take the Moyenne Corniche — the middle of the three coastal roads, the one Hitchcock used in To Catch a Thief. The road climbs sharply out of Nice and within minutes the coast is below you. First stop: the belvedere above Mont Boron. The Belle Époque villas cling to the hillside here, half-hidden in pine and cypress — the old money of Nice, largely unknown to visitors. The view is back over the old port and the Lympia basin, with the curve of the Baie des Anges to the west. The second stop is the one people remember: the panorama above Villefranche-sur-Mer. You are now looking down at Villefranche-sur-Mer — the deep harbour, the medieval citadel, the ochre houses along the quay, all of it from 400 metres above. That geometry, fixes the geography of the morning in a way no map does. Cap Ferrat extends into the sea to the south. On clear mornings, Cap Martin and the Italian coast are visible beyond it. These two stops add fifteen to twenty minutes. They are not optional.

The view from 400 metres — Villefranche bay and Cap Ferrat

03
Eze Village
427 metres · 60–75 min · independently

Eze is a medieval village built on a volcanic rock at 427 metres above the sea. Pedestrian from the gate — we leave you there and wait at the car park below. The lanes are steep, cobbled, and largely unchanged since the thirteenth century. At the top: the Jardin Exotique (€7) built around the ruins of a château, with cacti, succulents, and a view that takes in Cap Ferrat, Monaco, and on clear days the Italian coast as far as Bordighera. On the way down, the Fragonard perfume factory at the foot of the village — worth a stop even if you are not shopping for perfume. The soaps, candles and scented oils make better souvenirs from the Côte d'Azur than a sack of lavender produced in China. Sixty to seventy-five minutes here; not a minute wasted.

Our recommendation: Take the right lane at the gate and climb to the Hôtel Chèvre d'Or. Their terrace looks south over the sea — Cap Ferrat directly below, Monaco to the east. A coffee or a glass of champagne on that terrace, on a clear morning, is the best twenty minutes of this excursion.
04
Monaco
90–120 min · Port Hercule · Casino · Le Rocher

From Eze we descend to Monaco — the Principality, 2.02 square kilometres, ruled by the Grimaldi family since 1297. Port Hercule for the yachts: super-yachts moored stern-to, the scale of the boats against the buildings — and every May, the harbour becomes the Grand Prix circuit. The Casino de Monte-Carlo gardens and the Belle Époque façade — you can walk past without going in and still understand what this place is about. Then Le Rocher, the old town on the rock: the cathedral where Grace Kelly is buried, the palace changing of the guard at 11:55 if your timing allows, the view from the ramparts south over the sea. The return to Cannes is approximately sixty-five minutes on the A8. And there you are — back where you wanted to be. The phones contain several gigabytes of evidence, and in the considered opinion of everyone you brought along, you are the sort of person who can organise anything, perfectly.

Questions.
Plain answers.

Where do you collect us?

At your hotel, Airbnb, or any address in Cannes. We arrive at 09:00 — or earlier if you prefer. If you are staying outside Cannes — Mougins, Le Cannet, Mandelieu — we collect you there instead. WhatsApp us with the address: +33 6 69 96 22 72.

How long is the tour?

From six hours. Nice is thirty minutes from Cannes on the A8. Eze and Monaco add another hour of driving. The rest is time at each stop — unhurried, at your pace. We typically return to Cannes by late afternoon. If you prefer a shorter day, we adjust the route.

Do you accompany us inside Eze village?

No. We drop you at the village gate and wait with the car. Eze is better without a guide. We tell you where to find Chèvre d'Or before you go in.

Can we change the route on the day?

Yes. Skip Monaco and spend longer in Eze. Add a lunch stop on the harbour in Nice. Drive the Grande Corniche instead of the Moyenne. Visit Cap Ferrat on the way back. Every route is a suggestion — we design the day around what you want to see.

Is Eze difficult to walk?

Yes. Steep, cobbled, uneven lanes. Some sections have significant drops. Manageable for most people in reasonable health; not suitable for wheelchairs. Comfortable shoes strongly recommended.

Do you stop at the Fragonard perfume factory in Eze?

Yes, if you would like to. The Fragonard factory is at the foot of Eze village, directly on the private tour route. Entry and the guided tour are free. Even if perfume is not your thing, the soaps, candles and scented oils make excellent French Riviera souvenirs — well-made, reasonably priced, and considerably less predictable than lavender sachets.

Can we skip Nice and spend more time in Eze or Monaco?

Yes, and we often suggest it for guests who have already visited Nice. Skipping Nice saves forty minutes of driving from Cannes and gives a full extra hour in Eze or Monaco. The day is better for it. Mention it when you enquire.
