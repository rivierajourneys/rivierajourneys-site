The Excursion

Your cruise ship anchors in the bay — one of the deepest natural harbours in the Mediterranean. Villefranche-sur-Mer is a tender port: a small boat brings you to quai de l'Amiral Courbet, in the old village itself. We are there with a name board and a private Mercedes V-Class before your tender docks. This shore excursion covers the three essential stops on the French Riviera: Nice is six kilometres west, ten minutes by road. The Moyenne Corniche climbs directly from above the village to Eze. Monaco is east. The compact geography of Villefranche works entirely in your favour: less time in transit, more time at each stop.

The route.
Four stops, one day.

01
Nice
~10 min from Villefranche · 60–90 min ashore

We drive west along the Basse Corniche — Nice is six kilometres, ten minutes at most. The road hugs the coast below the villas of Cap de Nice; the old port appears ahead as you round the last bend, the Promenade des Anglais curving west beyond it along the Baie des Anges. Cours Saleya and the Vieux-Port, the flower and food market that runs every morning until noon. The old town behind it — narrow lanes, baroque churches, Italianate ochre facades, the light that made the Impressionists stay and return every winter. For a first visit, an hour here is the minimum. If you have been before, forty-five minutes in the market and we leave before the coaches arrive from the cruise port.

02
Moyenne Corniche
2 panoramic stops en route to Eze

From Nice we take the Moyenne Corniche — the middle of the three coastal roads, the one Hitchcock used in To Catch a Thief. The road climbs sharply out of Nice and within minutes the coast is below you. First stop: the belvedere above Mont Boron. The Belle Époque villas cling to the hillside here, half-hidden in pine and cypress — the old money of Nice, largely unknown to visitors. The view is back over the old port and the Lympia basin, with the curve of the Baie des Anges to the west. The second stop is the one people remember: the panorama above Villefranche-sur-Mer. You are now looking down at the harbour where your ship is still anchored — that geometry, ship and village and medieval citadel from 400 metres above, fixes the geography of the morning in a way no map does. Cap Ferrat extends into the sea to the south. On clear mornings, Cap Martin and the Italian coast are visible beyond it. These two stops add fifteen to twenty minutes. They are not optional.

The view from 400 metres — Villefranche bay, your ship below

03
Eze Village
427 metres · 60–75 min · independently

Eze is a medieval village built on a volcanic rock at 427 metres above the sea. Pedestrian from the gate — we leave you there and wait at the car park below. The lanes are steep, cobbled, and largely unchanged since the thirteenth century. At the top: the Jardin Exotique (€7) built around the ruins of a château, with cacti, succulents, and a view that takes in Cap Ferrat, Monaco, and on clear days the Italian coast as far as Bordighera. On the way down, the Fragonard perfume factory at the foot of the village — worth a stop even if you are not shopping for perfume. The soaps, candles and scented oils make better souvenirs from the Côte d'Azur than a sack of lavender produced in China. Sixty to seventy-five minutes here; not a minute wasted.

Our recommendation: Take the right lane at the gate and climb to the Hôtel Chèvre d'Or. Their terrace looks south over the sea — Cap Ferrat directly below, Monaco to the east. A coffee or a glass of champagne on that terrace, on a clear morning, is the best twenty minutes of this excursion.
04
Monaco
90–120 min · Port Hercule · Casino · Le Rocher

From Eze we descend to Monaco — the Principality, 2.02 square kilometres, ruled by the Grimaldi family since 1297. Port Hercule for the yachts: super-yachts moored stern-to, the scale of the boats against the buildings — and every May, the harbour becomes the Grand Prix circuit. The Casino de Monte-Carlo gardens and the Belle Époque façade — you can walk past without going in and still understand what this place is about. Then Le Rocher, the old town on the rock: the cathedral where Grace Kelly is buried, the palace changing of the guard at 11:55 if your timing allows, the view from the ramparts south over the sea. The return to Villefranche is approximately 20 minutes — the A8 east, straight back along the coast.

Questions.
Plain answers.

Where exactly do you meet us in Villefranche?

At quai de l'Amiral Courbet in Villefranche-sur-Mer — the tender landing pier, in the old village itself. We will be there with a name board before your tender docks. The quay is directly in front of the tender dock; the car is thirty seconds away. If you have any difficulty, WhatsApp us: +33 6 69 96 22 72.

How long is the excursion, and will it fit my ship's schedule?

The full Nice–Eze–Monaco route typically runs 5 to 7.5 hours from Villefranche, depending on your tender time and how long you wish to linger at each stop. Nice is only 6 kilometres — ten minutes each way — so the compact geography means more time at each stop, less in transit. We can trim or extend the itinerary to your ship's schedule. Eze is never cut short.

Do you accompany us inside Eze village?

No. We drop you at the village gate and wait with the car. Eze is better without a guide. We tell you where to find Chèvre d'Or before you go in.

What if the tender runs late or the ship changes its schedule?

We wait. There is no extra charge for tender delays. We stay in WhatsApp contact throughout the day and adapt the itinerary accordingly if the shore window is reduced. Villefranche ships anchor in the bay; tender delays are standard and we have built this into our planning from the start.

Is Eze difficult to walk?

Yes. Steep, cobbled, uneven lanes. Some sections have significant drops. Manageable for most people in reasonable health; not suitable for wheelchairs. Comfortable shoes strongly recommended.

Do you stop at the Fragonard perfume factory in Eze?

Yes, if you would like to. The Fragonard factory is at the foot of Eze village, directly on the shore excursion route. Entry and the guided tour are free. Even if perfume is not your thing, the soaps, candles and scented oils make excellent French Riviera souvenirs — well-made, reasonably priced, and considerably less predictable than lavender sachets.

Can we skip Nice and spend more time in Eze or Monaco?

Yes, and we often suggest it for guests who have already visited Nice. From Villefranche, skipping Nice saves twenty minutes of driving and gives a full extra hour in Eze. The day is better for it. Mention it when you enquire.

Which cruise ships stop at Villefranche-sur-Mer?

Villefranche receives cruise ships from Royal Caribbean, Celebrity Cruises, Oceania, Azamara, Silversea, Regent Seven Seas, and others. The bay is deep enough for the largest vessels. Most ships anchor between April and November, with peak cruise season in May, June, September and October. Check our Villefranche cruise schedule for exact dates.

Is Villefranche a tender port?

Yes. Villefranche-sur-Mer is a tender port — cruise ships anchor in the bay and passengers reach shore by tender boat. The tender ride takes about five minutes to quai de l'Amiral Courbet. We meet you directly at the tender dock with a private Mercedes V-Class. Tender delays are common; we always wait at no extra charge.
