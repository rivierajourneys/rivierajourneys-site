Nice, Èze and Monaco in one day.

Nice, Èze and Monaco sit within about twenty kilometres of one another, linked by the three Corniche roads that climb from the sea to the ridge above the principality. It is the classic Riviera day: a medieval village in the hills, Monaco below, and two countries before dinner. This guide sets out what each place asks of your time, three sample itineraries for five hours, seven hours and a full day, and an honest account of doing it by train, by bus, by taxi or by private car.

The route, and the three Corniches

Three roads run east from Nice, stacked one above another. The Basse Corniche, at sea level, threads the seaside towns — Villefranche, Beaulieu, Cap-d’Ail. The Moyenne Corniche, midway up, is the one that passes Èze village and carries the views most people picture. The Grande Corniche runs highest of all, past La Turbie and the Trophy of Augustus, with broad panoramas over Monaco and the coast.

In practice we take the Moyenne Corniche up to Èze and the coast road through the bays; on a private tour, if time allows, we can add a stretch of the Grande Corniche for the high view over Monaco. Driving all three in full is better kept for a separate, unhurried day — a circuit of the Corniches in its own right, rather than folded into this one.

Nice

Nice is always part of the day. The Promenade des Anglais runs the length of the Baie des Anges; a short walk through Vieux Nice, the old town, takes in the ochre lanes and the morning market on Cours Saleya. For the wide view over the city and the bay, we climb by road up Mont Boron, the green headland east of the port. How much of this you do depends on the hours in hand — the Promenade and a turn through the old town fit even a short day.

Èze

Èze is a medieval village on a cone of rock about 427 metres above the sea, its lanes too narrow for cars and arranged in tightening circles up to the Jardin Exotique at the summit, where on a clear day the view runs from Saint-Jean-Cap-Ferrat toward Italy. A Fragonard perfumery sits at the foot. Allow an hour to ninety minutes for the climb through the village and the garden, longer with lunch. The one practical catch is access — see the transport note below.

Monaco

Monaco divides into two parts that each want time. Le Rocher, the old town on the rock, holds the Prince’s Palace, the cathedral where Grace Kelly is buried, and the Oceanographic Museum; the changing of the guard takes place outside the palace at about 11.55 each morning. Monte-Carlo, across the harbour, is the Belle Époque quarter of the Casino, the Place du Casino and the Hôtel de Paris. Seeing both on foot takes the better part of three hours; one half alone, around ninety minutes.

Three itineraries

Five hours. The Promenade des Anglais and a turn through Vieux Nice, then up to Èze for the village and the view, and down to Monaco for one half — Le Rocher, with the changing of the guard if the timing falls right. One perched village, one side of Monaco.

Seven hours. The standard day. Nice with the climb to Mont Boron, Èze in full, both halves of Monaco, and lunch; a stretch of the Grande Corniche for the high view if the day runs to it.

A full day. All of the above without the clock — more time in Nice and Monaco, the Jardin Exotique at Èze, and a Corniche photo circuit; or extend east to Menton and the Italian border.

What to cut when time is short: the Mont Boron climb goes first, then one half of Monaco, then the Jardin Exotique at Èze — the Promenade, the old town, the village and one side of Monaco stay.

Train, bus, taxi or private car

By train, the coastal TER links Nice-Ville to Monaco-Monte-Carlo in about twenty-five minutes, cheap and frequent — but it does not reach Èze village. The station marked Èze-sur-Mer is at sea level; the village is the 427 metres above it, reached either on foot by the steep Nietzsche path, the better part of an hour’s climb, or by an infrequent connecting bus. The coastal bus, line 600, runs the same shore but is slower and crowded in season.

A taxi can cover a single leg, but for a full day across Nice, Èze and Monaco it is an expensive way to travel — the meter or a negotiated fare running through every stop — and few drivers speak much English, fewer still can tell you anything about the region or the places worth stopping for.

A private car is what links the four in one unbroken day. It takes the Corniches that public transport does not, stops at the viewpoints, delivers you to the foot of Èze rather than the bottom of the hill, and — for cruise passengers — returns you to the ship before the all-aboard time. We run it as a private day tour for up to seven guests, the price for the vehicle rather than per head.

Questions and answers

Can you see Nice, Èze and Monaco in one day?

Yes. The three sit within about twenty kilometres, linked by the Corniche roads. Five hours covers one perched village and one half of Monaco; seven hours is comfortable for Èze, both halves of Monaco and lunch.

Do you need a car to reach Èze village?

Effectively, yes. The train stops at Èze-sur-Mer, at sea level; the village is 427 metres above, reached on foot by the steep Nietzsche path or by an infrequent bus. A private car delivers you to the village itself.

What time is the changing of the guard in Monaco?

At about 11.55 each morning, outside the Prince’s Palace on Le Rocher. Plan the Monaco half of the day around it if you would like to watch.
