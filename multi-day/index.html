Some places do not fit into a day. The coast east of Cannes runs without interruption into Liguria and on to Tuscany; north-west, the motorway gives out after an hour and Provence begins. A multi-day journey is the same arrangement as a day tour extended over a week: the same vehicle, the same party, no group departures and no fixed schedule. We drive, you do not, and the pace is set by whoever is in the back.

Every journey is designed individually — the route, the overnight towns, the stops between them. The five itineraries below are not a catalogue but a frame: the journeys we have found most rewarding, most asked for and most comfortably driven. Any of them can be shortened, extended, reversed or redrawn around what you want to see. Pricing is on enquiry, because the total depends on the days, the distance and the hotels rather than on a rate card.

Why begin here

The right place to start, or to finish

Most of our multi-day guests are arriving from far away — the United States, Australia, Asia. Flying that distance for two days on a beach makes little sense. Flying that distance and then seeing a country properly does.

A real journey
Point-to-point flights collapse a country into airports: you board in one place and step out in another, and everything between is missed. Travelling by road is the opposite. The country unrolls at ground level — the coast giving way to the hills, France giving way to Italy — and the days between the cities become the journey rather than the gaps in it.
The geography
The Riviera sits exactly where such a journey wants to begin or end. Liguria, Tuscany, Provence, the Rhône valley — all of them lie within short daily legs of Cannes, so the route builds naturally in either direction without long transit days. You feel the distance without losing days to it.
The logistics
One flight in, and the rest of the machinery disappears. No tickets, no connections, no luggage to check and reclaim, no transfers to arrange in each new city. We meet you at the airport, and every morning after that the car is waiting outside the hotel. You have breakfast, take your coffee, come down when you are ready and travel on.
The journeys

Five routes, and variations

Frameworks rather than fixed itineraries: the journeys we have run most often, each open to being shortened, extended or redrawn. The days can be added to, the order changed, the overnight stops moved.

5 – 7 days · Italy
Cannes · Genova · Cinque Terre · Florence. The established route and the one people talk about afterwards. Genova is a serious city that almost no visitor to the Riviera sees. The Cinque Terre wants a full day and an early start. Florence closes the week.
3 – 5 days · Italy
Cannes · Genova · Portofino. The shorter Ligurian version. Sanremo and the Italian border villages on the way out, the Gulf of Tigullio at the far end, and a return along the coast rather than the motorway.
3 – 5 days · Provence
Cannes · Aix · Avignon · Valensole. Inland Provence, with the Valensole plateau in flower from late June to mid July. Outside those weeks the route still works, and the villages are quieter for it.
5 – 7 days · France
Cannes · Avignon · Lyon. North through Provence and the Rhône valley, with the wine country either side of the river and Lyon at the end of it.
7 – 10 days · France
Cannes · Lyon · Paris. The full length of the country by road rather than by train, with Burgundy in the middle of it. The longest journey we arrange as a single itinerary.
How it works

What is arranged, and by whom

The vehicle
A Mercedes V-Class for the full period, with room for seven people and luggage for a week. Fuel, tolls, parking and border formalities are covered.
The hotels
We propose the overnight towns and can book the rooms, or we build the driving around bookings you have already made. The choice of town matters more than the choice of hotel within it.
The pace
Three to four hours of driving in a day at most, and usually less. Short legs with the reason for the journey in between them.
The price
One total for the journey, quoted before you commit to anything. It depends on days, distance and the standard of accommodation, which is why there is no useful figure on this page.

Questions. Plain answers.

How is a multi-day journey priced?

On enquiry. The figure depends on the number of days, the distance covered and the standard of hotel chosen, so there is no useful single price. We quote one total for the journey before you commit to anything.

Do you arrange the hotels?

We can, or you can. Most parties prefer us to propose the overnight stops, because the choice of town matters more than the choice of hotel within it. If you have your own bookings already, we build the driving around them.

How much driving is there in a day?

Three to four hours at most, and usually less. A journey that spends six hours a day in the vehicle is a transfer with sightseeing attached, not a journey. We plan short legs with the stops in between them.

Can we change the route once we have started?

Within reason, yes. Hotels are booked in advance and set the shape of the week, but what happens between them is open. If a town holds you longer than expected, the next leg absorbs it.

What is included in a multi-day journey?

The vehicle and the driving for the full period, fuel, tolls, parking and border formalities. Accommodation, meals and entrance fees are separate, whether we book them or you do.

How many people can travel?

Up to seven in one Mercedes V-Class, with luggage for a week. Larger parties travel in two vehicles on the same route.
